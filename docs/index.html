Search Intelligence Refresh Opportunity Scoring: Machine Learning Prioritization of Search Traffic Decay

A.S.M. Arsad Ahamed Safone
FlyRank Machine Learning Internship Program • Lane 2 (Content Refresh Scoring)
Date: August 2026
Repository: github.com/AsmSafone/ML-Internship
Abstract

Out of thousands of published content items across client sites, search visibility naturally decays over time while human editorial review capacity remains strictly limited. This project addresses the question of how to score and prioritize decaying pages to direct editorial updates where traffic recovery potential is highest. Using FlyRank's 79M-row warehouse performance dataset, we engineered a 10-feature opportunity frame and evaluated rule baselines against Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest models on client-holdout splits (GroupShuffleSplit on client_id). On unseen client holdouts, Random Forest achieved a Precision@50 of 0.680 (and 1.000 in the final blended queue), representing a 2.2x to 3.3x lift over the transparent rule baseline (0.300). The final system outputs an automated Content Action Playbook with explicit reason codes (e.g. CTR Optimization, Content Depth Expansion, Page 1 Protection), providing scalable decision-support for content strategy operations.

Keywords— Content Refresh Scoring, Machine Learning Prioritization, Organic Search Decay, Grouped Validation, FlyRank Warehouse Dataset.

1. Problem Framing

Organic search traffic is non-stationary: content published on digital properties experiences performance decay due to shifting search intent, competitor content updates, and search engine algorithm iterations. However, editorial teams face severe bandwidth constraints, typically capable of updating only 50 to 100 pages per month out of enterprise catalogues containing tens of thousands of URLs.

  • Decision Supported: Operational prioritization of monthly editorial review resources (ranking candidate pages for top-\(K\) monthly review).
  • Unit of Analysis: One row = one pseudonymized content item (page) for a given client over a trailing 90-day aggregation window.
  • Output: A prioritized action queue with composite opportunity scores (\(0 \text{ to } 100\)), primary reason codes, and recommended editorial actions.
  • Action Taken: Content editors review the top candidates and execute specific updates (updating outdated facts, expanding depth, or optimizing titles/meta descriptions).
  • Cost of Errors:
    • False Positive: Wasted editorial hours spent rewriting content that did not need a refresh.
    • False Negative: Unnoticed search visibility decay leading to compounding organic traffic drops and revenue loss.
  • Why Machine Learning Beats Static Rules: Hand-written rules cannot dynamically scale or handle non-linear interactions across 10+ search visibility, position tier, CTR, and engagement metrics across clients with varying history depths.

2. Data Safety & Provenance

All experiments were conducted on the FlyRank Pseudonymized Warehouse Release (v20260703) hosted on Hugging Face.

  • Tables Utilized: fact_content_daily_performance (78.8M daily fact rows), dim_clients (104 clients), and dim_content (519,606 content items).
  • Deliberate Feature Exclusions (Leakage Prevention):
    • trend_direction and trend_pct: Excluded from feature matrices because they are the direct source of the target label is_declining_label.
    • Downstream product decision flags (health_score, priority_score, action_type): Excluded to prevent learning past rules rather than organic search behavior.
    • Pseudonymous IDs (content_id, client_id): Retained strictly for relational joins and grouped validation splits.
  • Privacy Audit: Zero raw URLs, domain names, client names, or private search queries are present in any committed code, datasets, or outputs.

3. Transparent Baseline Rule

To establish an honest benchmark, we constructed a human-readable 4-part composite baseline score combining search demand, content staleness, position opportunity, and word count depth gap:

$$\text{Score}_{\text{baseline}} = 100 \times \Big( 0.40 \cdot \text{Visibility} + 0.30 \cdot \text{FreshnessRisk} + 0.25 \cdot \text{PositionOpp} + 0.05 \cdot \text{DepthGap} \Big)$$

The baseline generates six rule-based reason codes: stale_visible_page, declining_with_demand, thin_visible_page, page_one_decay_risk, low_ctr_visible_page, and low_engagement_visible_page.

Baseline Performance (Client-Holdout Test Set):

  • Precision@50: 0.300 (Base Rate of Decline: 0.511)
  • Average Precision (AP): 0.487
  • ROC AUC: 0.496

4. Model & Feature Engineering

We evaluated three model architectures against the baseline: Logistic Regression (calibrated linear model), Decision Tree (depth=5), and Random Forest (100 trees, max_depth=10).

Feature Vector (10 signals): log_impressions_90d, log_clicks_90d, log_sessions_90d, word_count, days_since_last_update, content_age_days, avg_position, ctr, engagement_rate, and scroll_rate. Heavy-tailed count metrics were log-transformed (\(\log(1+x)\)) to stabilize variance.

Target Definition: Binary target is_declining_label = (trend_direction == "down"), evaluating whether trailing 30-day search impressions dropped by \(>20\%\) relative to the preceding period.

5. Evaluation & Leakage Audit

Validation design dictates model trustworthiness. A standard random train/test split allows pages from the same client to appear in both splits, enabling models to memorize client-specific baseline traffic levels rather than learning general search decay patterns. To enforce strict evaluation, we used GroupShuffleSplit on client_id (80% train clients, 20% holdout clients).

Table 1: Model Comparison vs. Rule Baseline on Client-Holdout Test Set (Base Rate = 0.511)
Method / Model Precision@50 Average Precision ROC AUC
Rule Baseline (Transparent Score) 0.300 0.487 0.496
Logistic Regression 0.780 0.626 0.628
Decision Tree (depth=5) 0.480 0.580 0.603
Random Forest (100 Trees, Final Model) 0.680 0.595 0.604

Leakage Audit Findings: Evaluating the same Random Forest model under a naïve random split yielded ROC AUC 0.764 / Precision@50 0.940. Enforcing client-grouped holdout splits dropped ROC AUC by 0.159 points to 0.604, revealing that 16 ROC AUC points in naïve evaluation stemmed from client-level memorization leakage.

6. Interpretation & Feature Importances

Feature importance analysis from the Random Forest model confirms the structural drivers of search opportunity scoring:

  1. log_impressions_90d (26.6%): Search impression exposure is the primary determinant of decay severity and potential impact.
  2. avg_position (20.0%): Ranking in Page 1 (positions 1–10) vs. Page 2 dictates click sensitivity.
  3. content_age_days (15.8%): Temporal age increases vulnerability to intent drift.
  4. word_count (10.3%): Content depth acts as a stabilizing buffer against ranking volatility.

False Positive Analysis: High-confidence false positives primarily occurred on high-impression content items subject to seasonal search demand shifts rather than actual quality degradation.

7. Recommendation & Content Action Playbook

We combine learned model probabilities with our transparent rule baseline to construct the final prioritized refresh score:

$$\text{Score}_{\text{final}} = 100 \times \Big( 0.70 \cdot P_{\text{model}} + 0.30 \cdot \frac{\text{Score}_{\text{baseline}}}{100} \Big)$$

Reason codes trigger automated editorial action mapping:

  • low_ctr_visible_page → Rewrite Title & Meta Description (CTR Optimization)
  • thin_visible_page → Expand Content Depth & Add Comprehensive Sections
  • stale_visible_page → Update Outdated Information & Refresh Publication Date
  • page_one_decay_risk → Protect Page 1 Rank (Internal Linking & Schema Refresh)
  • low_engagement_visible_page → Improve On-Page Engagement & UX Formatting

Final Queue Quality: Evaluating the top 50 pages in the final blended queue yields a Precision@50 of 1.000 (100% of top candidates are true declining pages).
